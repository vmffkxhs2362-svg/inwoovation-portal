Optimization of Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD) for Greenhouse Tomato Yield, Transpiration Kinetics & Calcium Allocation

Research Review & Engineering Guide: Inwoovation AgTech Research Lab | Updated: September 2026 | Keywords: Leaf-to-Air VPD, Solanum lycopersicum, Blossom-End Rot, Xylem Mass-Flow

Executive Abstract & Biophysical Overview

Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD) represents the absolute drying power of the atmosphere, defined as the difference between the saturation vapor pressure inside the saturated sub-stomatal leaf cavity (VPsat(T_leaf)) and the actual vapor pressure of bulk greenhouse air (VPair(T_air, RH)). In protected cultivation of tomatoes (Solanum lycopersicum), maintaining leaf-to-air VPD strictly within the target range of 0.80 to 1.20 kPa is essential for optimizing the delicate balance between carbon assimilation (photosynthesis) and xylem transpirational mass flow.

1. Physiological Impact on Stomatal Conductance & Net Photosynthesis

When ambient VPD drops below 0.50 kPa (excessively humid, stagnant conditions), the vapor pressure gradient across the stomatal pore diminishes. While guard cells remain turgid and stomata are physically open, the driving gradient for transpiration evaporates. In contrast, when VPD exceeds 1.40 kPa (hot, arid conditions), the rate of leaf water loss outpaces root water uptake. Guard cells lose turgor pressure, triggering abscisic acid (ABA) signaling and active stomatal closure. This throttles internal intercellular CO₂ concentration (C_i), reducing net photosynthetic carbon assimilation by 25% to 40%.

2. Calcium Partitioning & Blossom-End Rot (BER) Pathophysiology

Unlike phloem-mobile ions like potassium (K⁺) and nitrogen, calcium (Ca²⁺) is strictly phloem-immobile and moves almost exclusively through the xylem apoplast via transpirational volume flux (Adams & Ho, 1993). In low-VPD environments (< 0.40 kPa), total canopy transpiration drops dramatically. The developing distal fruit tissue, which has a very low surface-area-to-volume ratio and minimal stomatal density compared to expanded leaves, receives negligible xylem sap flow. The resulting localized calcium deficiency at the blossom-end of rapidly expanding green fruit leads to cell membrane disintegration, cellular necrosis, and the physiological disorder known as Blossom-End Rot (BER).

3. Commercial Climate Computer Actuator Coordination

Modern greenhouse environmental computers (Priva Connext, Hoogendoorn i-Gro, Ridder HortiMaX) control VPD through an integrated tri-variable loop:

  • When VPD is Too Low (< 0.60 kPa): The computer introduces sensible heat via the lower crop heating loop (minimum pipe temperature 40°C - 50°C) while simultaneously opening lee-side roof vents by 2% to 5%. This drives moist greenhouse air out through buoyancy convection while drying the canopy surface.
  • When VPD is Too High (> 1.30 kPa): High-pressure fogging lines operating at 50 to 70 bar atomize water droplets (< 15 microns), evaporating instantly in the air to absorb sensible heat and elevate relative humidity without wetting foliage.
