Commercial Vertical Farming HVAC Sizing: Dehumidification Latent Loads & Microclimate Fluid Dynamics

📅 2026-08-12 🏷️ HVAC & CEA Engineering ✍️ Inwoovation Lab Research Team

1. Core Pillars of Indoor Vertical Farming HVAC: Latent Dehumidification & Microclimate Airflow

Unlike conventional greenhouses that exchange moisture through solar-driven natural ventilation, enclosed vertical farms operate in sealed indoor thermal envelopes. Virtually 95–98% of all irrigation water delivered to the rootzone is transpired by the crop canopy into the indoor atmosphere as latent moisture. Failure to accurately size precision industrial HVAC and dehumidification systems leads to catastrophic humidity spikes (RH > 90%), fungal proliferation, and severe tip-burn.

2. Mathematical Formulation of Daily Canopy Latent Transpiration Loads

The required cooling and dehumidification capacity must directly counterbalance electrical lighting heat dissipation (sensible load) and plant transpiration (latent load):

m_transpiration = Daily Water Delivered × Transpiration Ratio (0.95) [kg/day]
Q_latent = (m_transpiration ÷ 24) × h_fg [kW] (h_fg ≈ 2450 kJ/kg)
Q_sensible = ∑ P_LED × (1 - η_PAR) + Q_motors + Q_ambient [kW]

For a facility irrigating 5,000 L of water per day, the continuous latent dehumidification duty alone exceeds \(140 ext{ kW}\) of cooling energy, requiring dedicated reheat or desiccant-based moisture extraction systems.

3. Rack Air Velocity Sizing (0.3–0.5 m/s) to Eliminate Thermal Micro-Stratification

  • Thermal Micro-Pocketing: Multi-tier grow racks stack high-density LED fixtures, generating localized convective heat plumes directly beneath upper grow trays. Without active ducting, air temperature differentials can exceed 4–6°C across vertical tiers.
  • Target Air Velocity: Deploy pressurized manifold ducts or micro-circulation EC fans providing uniform air velocities between \(0.3 ext{--}0.5 ext{ m/s}\) directly across the plant canopy.
  • Tip-Burn Eradication: Sufficient airflow disrupts the stagnant boundary layer of inner young leaves, accelerating transpirational calcium pull and eliminating calcium-deficiency tip-burn in leafy greens.
