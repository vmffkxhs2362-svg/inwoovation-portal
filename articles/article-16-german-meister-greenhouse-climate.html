German Master Grower (Gärtnermeister) Greenhouse Climate Steering Strategy for Solanaceae

📅 2026-08-29 🏷️ Climate Steering & Engineering ✍️ Inwoovation AgTech Research

1. The 24-Hour Radiation-Temperature Integration (RTI)

In modern German and Dutch glasshouse horticulture (Straelen / Venlo standard), crop development rate is fundamentally driven by the 24-hour average temperature (24h-Mitteltemperatur) rather than instantaneous peak temperatures. When daily solar radiation sum (\(J/cm^2\)) is high, Master Growers increase day temperature targets to maximize assimilation, while lowering night setpoints to save heating energy and prevent excessive night respiration.

T_24h_target = T_base + (Radiation_Sum_Daily × k_radiation_factor) [°C]
RTI_balance = ∫ (T_actual - T_target) dt over 24-hour cycle

2. Pre-Night Temperature Drop (Der Vornacht-DIP)

A signature technique of German Gärtnermeister practice is the Pre-Night Drop (Vornacht-Absenkung). Approximately 1 to 2 hours before sunset, ventilation screens and pipe temperatures are modulated to drop greenhouse temperature rapidly by 3°C to 5°C. This promotes:

  • Photosynthate Translocation: Rapid export of sucrose from tomato leaves to developing fruit trusses without burning energy on vegetative growth.
  • Stem Internode Control: Negative DIF (Day Temperature − Night Temperature) inhibits stem elongation, resulting in robust, compact crop architectures.
  • Condensation Avoidance: Drops the air temperature before the roof glass cools below the dew point, preventing Botrytis fungal germination.

3. 3-Way Mixing Valve Pipe Rail Heat Modulation

High-tech heating systems utilize low-temperature bottom rail pipes (35°C–55°C) to generate micro-convection currents directly within the crop canopy. By modulating a 3-way motorized mixing valve with proportional-integral-derivative (PID) control, heating responsiveness is decoupled from main boiler delivery temperatures.
