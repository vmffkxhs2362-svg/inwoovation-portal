Biophysical Thermodynamics of Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD) & Canopy Micro-Climatology

Authored by Dr. vmffkxhs2362 • Published August 5, 2026 • Inwoovation CEA Biophysics Division

1. Mathematical Formulation of Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD)

In Controlled Environment Agriculture (CEA), leaf-to-air Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD) governs the thermodynamic driving force for transpiration, stomatal conductance (\(g_s\)), and mass-flow xylem translocation of non-mobile cations such as Calcium (\(Ca^{2+}\)) and Magnesium (\(Mg^{2+}\)).

The saturation vapor pressure (\(VP_{sat}\)) at temperature \(T\) (°C) is defined by the Tetens equation:

VP_sat(T) = 0.61078 × exp( (17.27 × T) / (T + 237.3) ) [kPa]

The air Vapor Pressure Deficit (\(VPD_{air}\)) is calculated from relative humidity (\(RH\), %):

VPD_air = VP_sat(T_air) × (1 - RH / 100) [kPa]

2. Leaf Boundary Layer Resistance (\(r_b\)) and Stomatal Conductance

Transpiration flux (\(E\), \( ext{mol H}_2 ext{O m}^{-2} ext{ s}^{-1}\)) is governed by Fick's Law of Diffusion, inversely proportional to total leaf resistance (\(r_{total} = r_s + r_b\)):

  • Vegetative Steering (VPD 0.6 - 0.9 kPa): Maintains guard cell turgor, optimizing stomatal aperture for maximum sub-stomatal \(CO_2\) concentration (\(C_i\)).
  • Generative Steering (VPD 1.1 - 1.5 kPa): Accelerates xylem sap velocity, driving transpirational calcium flow to apical meristems and preventing blossom-end rot (BER) and tip-burn.
