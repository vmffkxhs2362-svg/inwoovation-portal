📚 Power-to-Heat (20 kV Elektrokessel), Negative Spot Pricing & Buffer Tank Engineering

Across the European interconnected grid (EPEX SPOT in Germany, the Netherlands, Belgium, and France), rapid expansion of utility-scale solar photovoltaics and North Sea offshore wind has structurally transformed commercial horticulture from a passive fossil-gas consumer into an active flexible grid-balancing asset. Between April and September, midday solar surges routinely push day-ahead auction prices between 11:00 and 15:00 CET into deep negative territory (-30 to -110 €/MWh). Conversely, sunset ramps between 18:00 and 21:00 CET trigger steep evening price spikes exceeding +170 €/MWh.

Modern Venlo glasshouses exploit this volatility by decoupling heat generation from instantaneous crop demand. Instead of firing natural gas boilers at night when crop heating demand peaks, an autonomous Model Predictive Controller (MPC) activates a medium-voltage 20 kV electrode boiler (Power-to-Heat Elektrokessel, thermal conversion efficiency η = 99.0%) alongside an industrial ammonia (R717) or propane (R290) heat pump during negative-price solar or wind windows. The resulting 88–92 °C hot water is stored inside an insulated atmospheric steel buffer tank (Pufferspeicher, typically 500 to 1,000 m3 per hectare) and discharged smoothly through low-temperature pipe-rail networks overnight.

1. Medium-Voltage 20 kV Electrode Boilers vs. Resistance Heaters

Above 2.0 MWel, low-voltage 400 V metallic resistance heating elements suffer from excessive copper busbar currents, thermal fatigue, and slow switching transients. Industrial 10 kV to 20 kV electrode boilers pass alternating current directly through conductivity-controlled boiler water (typically 250–600 µS/cm trisodium phosphate dosing), eliminating metallic heating element burn-out and achieving 0-to-100% ramp times under 30 seconds for lucrative secondary frequency-restoration reserve (aFRR / Regelenergie) prequalification.

2. § 19 Abs. 2 StromNEV Grid Fee Optimization & LFP-BESS Co-Location

Spot price arbitrage succeeds only when network charges (Netzentgelte) do not erase wholesale discounts. By avoiding transformer draw during the grid operator's published high-load windows (Hochlastzeitfenster) via behind-the-meter Lithium Iron Phosphate (LFP) battery storage (89% round-trip efficiency, 6,000+ cycle life), commercial greenhouses qualify for atypical grid usage under § 19 Abs. 2 Satz 1 StromNEV, slashing capacity charges by up to 80%. Simultaneously, LFP-BESS shifts zero-cost midday megawatt-hours into evening supplemental LED photoperiods.

Combining federal and municipal decarbonization grants compresses turnkey P2H + Heat Pump + BESS payback from 5.8 years to 2.6–3.4 years. ❓ Frequently Asked Engineering Questions (Technical FAQ)

Why must calcium nitrate and sulfate/phosphate fertilizers be separated into A and B stock tanks?

Mixing concentrated calcium ions (Ca²⁺) with sulfate (SO₄²⁻) or orthophosphate (H₂PO₄⁻) in the same stock tank causes immediate precipitation of insoluble gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O) and calcium phosphate once the solubility product (Ksp) is exceeded.

How do you calculate the required acid volume for hydroponic bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻) alkalinity neutralization?

Subtract target residual buffer alkalinity (typically 0.5 to 0.8 mmol/L HCO₃⁻ for pH 5.6–5.8 stability) from raw water bicarbonate concentration, then stoichiometry-match with nitric acid (HNO₃) or phosphoric acid (H₃PO₄) normality.

What drain percentage and slab EC dryback should be targeted in rockwool and cocopeat substrates?

During high-radiation summer days, target 25% to 35% daily drainage fraction with an overnight volumetric water content (VWC) dryback of 8% to 12% for vegetative steering, or 12% to 18% dryback for generative steering.
