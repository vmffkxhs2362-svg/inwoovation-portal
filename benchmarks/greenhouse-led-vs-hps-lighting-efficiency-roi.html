💡 Photobiological & Thermodynamic Analysis: LED (3.5–3.8 µmol/J) vs. 1000W DE HPS (1.8 µmol/J)

Supplemental assimilation lighting is the single largest electrical cost center in high-latitude commercial greenhouses producing winter tomatoes, cucumbers, sweet peppers, strawberries, cut roses, and medicinal cannabis. For decades, the industry standard was the 1,000 W double-ended (DE) High-Pressure Sodium (HPS) lamp operating on a 400 V high-frequency electronic ballast, achieving a photosynthetic photon efficacy (PPE) of 1.75 to 1.90 µmol/J. Modern horticultural light-emitting diodes (LEDs)—combining Gallium Nitride (GaN) blue/white pump diodes at 450 nm with Aluminum Gallium Indium Phosphide (AlGaInP) deep-red diodes at 660 nm—now achieve certified DLC V3.0 / ASABE S640 efficacies of 3.40 to 3.85 µmol/J.

1. Radiative Energy Partitioning & Canopy Leaf Temperature Balance

2. The Winter Thermal Compensation Effect (Boiler Offset Accounting)

When transitioning from 100% HPS to 100% LED in cold winter climates, removing $54\text{ W/m}^2$ of electrical heat input—specifically the $45\%$ downward NIR radiative beam from HPS reflectors—reduces radiative warming of the apical meristem. To maintain an identical 24-hour canopy meristem temperature and transpiration rate, the greenhouse climate computer must increase hydronic heating pipe temperatures, supplying roughly 35% to 45% of the removed electrical wattage as supplemental thermal heat ($+12\text{ to }+20\text{ W/m}^2$ during dark winter hours). Even after deducting this additional natural gas or heat-pump thermal cost, LED retrofits generate massive net savings because thermal energy ($\$0.03\text{–}\$0.06/\text{kWh}_{th}$) is 3 to 5 times cheaper than electrical energy ($\$0.12\text{–}\$0.25/\text{kWh}_e$).

4. Frequently Asked Engineering Questions (LED vs. HPS)

Should growers install a hybrid HPS + LED layout or go 100% full LED?
In unscreened legacy glasshouses with undersized heating pipes, a 50/50 hybrid checkerboard layout preserves half of the HPS radiative leaf warming while cutting power draw by 25%. However, in modern greenhouses equipped with double thermal screens and adequate tube-rail/grow-pipe capacity, 100% multi-channel LED toplighting with 5–8% far-red (730 nm) and dimmable drivers is superior, allowing dynamic DLI harvesting when spot electricity prices are negative or low and summer operation without overheating the canopy.
