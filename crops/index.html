📐 Biophysical Principles of Quantitative Crop Steering (Plant Balance & Assimilate Partitioning)

In modern Dutch and German high-tech horticulture (Gartenbau-Meister / Plant Empowerment), climate and irrigation computers do not merely maintain static temperature or humidity setpoints. Instead, growers steer the physiological balance between vegetative leaf/stem capacity (Source) and generative flower/fruit sink strength (Sink) across three coupled physical balances: the Energy Balance, the Water Balance, and the Assimilate (CO₂) Balance.

1. The Radiation-to-Temperature Ratio (RTR) & 24-Hour Thermal Integration

Plant photosynthesis is driven photochemically by Photosynthetically Active Radiation ($PAR$, 400–700 nm), which has a low temperature sensitivity ($Q_{10} \approx 1.1\text{–}1.3$ at elevated $\text{CO}_2$). Conversely, dark respiration, phloem loading, meristem cell division, and truss development rate follow enzymatic Arrhenius kinetics ($Q_{10} \approx 2.0$). Consequently, the 24-hour average greenhouse temperature ($T_{24h}$) must scale linearly with the Daily Light Integral ($DLI$, $\text{mol}\cdot\text{m}^{-2}\cdot\text{d}^{-1}$):

T_24h_target (°C) = T_base_crop + k_RTR × (DLI_actual / 10 mol·m⁻²·d⁻¹)

If $T_{24h}$ exceeds the assimilate supply supported by the day's $DLI$, the crop depletes non-structural carbohydrates via night respiration, producing thin stems, weak flower trusses, and blossom abortion. Conversely, if $T_{24h}$ is too low relative to $DLI$, sink translocation stalls and starch accumulates in chloroplasts, triggering feedback inhibition of Rubisco and purple leaf anthocyanin stress.
