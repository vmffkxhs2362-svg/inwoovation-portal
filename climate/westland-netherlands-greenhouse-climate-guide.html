📍 1. Geographic Microclimate & North Sea Maritime Buffer

The Westland region (51.99°N, 4.21°E, elevation 0 to -2 m NAP), often celebrated as the "Glass City" (Glazen Stad), is the historic global epicenter of high-tech Controlled Environment Agriculture (CEA). Bordered immediately by the North Sea to the west and the Port of Rotterdam to the south, Westland enjoys a strongly buffered maritime temperate climate (Köppen Cfb). Extreme summer heatwaves (>35°C) are rare due to cooling marine sea breezes, while severe winter freezes are mitigated by North Sea thermal inertia, resulting in a mean January temperature of +3.5°C and rare dips below -7°C.

However, the coastal latitude causes pronounced seasonal daylight asymmetry. Global horizontal irradiance peaks at 40–42 mol/m²/day in June under high sun angles, but collapses to less than 6.0 mol/m²/day in December under frequent maritime cloud cover and short 7.5-hour photoperiods. Consequently, commercial year-round crop production depends strictly on high-efficiency horticultural supplemental lighting and multi-layered climate screens.

🛡️ 3. DIN V 18599 Heating Physics & Trias Westland Geothermal

Heating design for modern 6.5m–7.5m post-height Venlo structures follows rigorous DIN V 18599 and NEN 3859 thermal modeling:

  • Unscreened Structural Heat Transfer ($U$-value): Standard single 4mm float glass exhibits an overall heat transfer coefficient of $U = 5.8\text{ W/m}^2\text{K}$. During design winter minimum conditions (-8°C external, +18°C internal, $\Delta T = 26\text{ K}$), peak heating load reaches 150–180 W/m².
  • Dual Thermal Screen Attenuation: Installing double horizontal screens—combining an ultra-transparent daytime energy screen (e.g., Ludvig Svensson Luxous 1147 FR, 47% energy saving) with a dense nighttime thermal/blackout screen (Obscura)—reduces overall $U$-value to $2.8–3.1\text{ W/m}^2\text{K}$, dropping peak heating requirements by 45% to 52%.
  • Deep Geothermal (Aardwarmte) Integration: The Trias Westland project extracts 85°C geothermal brine from Lower Cretaceous sandstone aquifers at 2.3 km depth. Supplying high-temperature base-load hot water to interconnected glasshouse clusters, it displaces over 35 million m³ of natural gas annually, transitioning Westland toward climate-neutral CEA.

🌿 4. Het Nieuwe Telen (HNT) & Humidity Deficit Control

Under the Dutch Het Nieuwe Telen (Next Generation Growing) philosophy developed by Wageningen University & Research (WUR Bleiswijk), greenhouses maintain active dehumidification with screens fully closed:

  • Mechanical Heat Recovery Dehumidifiers (DryGair / Air & Energy): Condense transpired plant moisture while returning the latent heat of vaporization ($2.45\text{ MJ/kg H}_2\text{O}$) into the greenhouse air, preventing Botrytis cinerea without cracking open ridge vents in cold weather.
  • Target Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD): Daytime canopy VPD is steered between 0.8 and 1.2 kPa for optimal stomatal conductance and calcium translocation into growing tips, while night VPD is maintained at 0.5 to 0.7 kPa to prevent gutation and blossom end rot.
