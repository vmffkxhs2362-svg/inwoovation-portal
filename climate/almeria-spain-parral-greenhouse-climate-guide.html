📍 1. Geographic Microclimate & "The Sea of Plastic" (Mar de Plástico)

The province of Almería (specifically the coastal plain of Campo de Dalías and Campo de Níjar, 36.84°N, 2.46°W, elevation 10–120 m ASL) hosts over 32,000 hectares of interconnected polyethylene-clad greenhouses. Sheltered from cold northern European continental air masses by the Sierra Nevada mountain range and flanked by the warm Mediterranean Sea, Almería boasts Europe's highest annual sunshine duration (>3,000 hours/year) and lowest rainfall (<220 mm/year).

Remarkably, NASA satellite studies (Campra et al.) have documented a localized albedo cooling effect across Campo de Dalías: the vast expanse of white plastic roofs reflects solar radiation back into space, reducing average surface air temperatures by approximately -0.3°C per decade even while surrounding Mediterranean regions have warmed.

💡 2. Monthly Solar DLI Curves & Winter Photosynthetic Dominance

Unlike Central or Northern European greenhouses that suffer severe winter light starvation, Almería's winter minimum solar DLI remains remarkably high at 18 to 23 mol/m²/day under unshaded conditions. This natural abundance allows high-density fruit production of sweet peppers, tomatoes, and cucumbers throughout November–March without expensive supplemental LED assimilation lighting.

🛡️ 3. Structural Mechanics & Whitewashing (Blanqueo)

Protected agriculture in Almería relies primarily on the traditional Parral and modernized Raspa y Amagado greenhouse architectures:

  • Wire-Mesh Tensioned Framing: Built with vertical galvanized steel pipes (or treated wood posts), rigid perimeter cables, and an interwoven upper and lower steel wire grid sandwiching 200-micron thermal anti-drip polyethylene sheeting. The flexible tensile structure withstands fierce Mediterranean Poniente and Levante gale winds.
  • Whitewashing (Blanqueo con cal): In May, growers spray a slurry of calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$) and fixatives across the roof exterior. This reduces solar radiation transmission by 40–55%, lowering internal canopy temperatures by 4–7°C during peak summer months. Autumn rainfall naturally degrades the binder, restoring full light transmission for winter.
  • Insect Exclusion Screens (Malla antitrips): Mandatory 50-mesh (or 10×20 threads/cm²) side-wall screens block vector pests (Bemisia tabaci, Frankliniella occidentalis), enabling 100% biological control (Nesidiocoris tenuis, Orius laevigatus) across 26,000+ hectares of integrated pest management (IPM).

🧪 4. Seawater Desalination (SWRO) & Nutrient Dynamics

Due to severe aquifer overdraft and marine saltwater intrusion in the Campo de Dalías aquifer, modern Almerían agriculture operates on advanced desalination infrastructure:

  • SWRO Blending: State-of-the-art reverse osmosis plants (Acuamed Campo de Dalías, 30 hm³/year capacity; Carboneras plant) provide pure permeate water ($EC < 0.3\text{ mS/cm}$). This is blended with brackish well water to deliver an initial irrigation base $EC$ of 0.8–1.2 mS/cm.
  • Enarenado Substrate & Perlite Hydroponics: Historical production utilizes enarenado (a layered soil profile of basal clay, intermediate manure, and a 10 cm quartz sand mulch that breaks capillary rise and blocks evaporation). Modern expansions deploy closed perlite bags or rockwool slabs with precision drip fertigation, recirculating drainage fractions to conserve water in hyper-arid conditions.

🏢 5. Cluster Cooperatives & Export Scale

Almería serves as Europe's winter vegetable basket, supplying over 3.5 million tons of produce annually through leading grower cooperatives including CASI (Cooperativa Agrícola San Isidro, Europe's largest tomato auction), Vicasol, Unica Group, and Agroponiente.
