🌍 Comparative Agro-Climatology & CEA Engineering Methodology

Designing a commercially viable controlled environment agriculture (CEA) facility requires matching structural typology—such as high-tech Dutch Venlo glasshouses, semi-closed positive-pressure houses, or Mediterranean Parral/Raspa y Amagado polyethylene structures—to local solar radiation and psychrometric boundary conditions. This Global Ag-Climate Atlas synthesizes 40 major horticultural clusters spanning from 56.0°N in Northern Europe to 43.5°S in Oceania.

1. Solar Daily Light Integral (DLI) & Structural Transmittance

Outdoor Daily Light Integral quantifies the cumulative photosynthetically active radiation (PAR, 400–700 nm) delivered per square meter per day (mol/m²/day). Because greenhouse superstructure frames, gutters, glazing panes, and retractable screen packages intercept 22–35% of incident photons, engineers multiply outdoor DLI by the structural hemispherical transmittance coefficient ($\tau_{\text{PAR}} \approx 0.65\text{–}0.78$) to determine true canopy DLI. High-latitude maritime and continental zones (e.g., Westland at 52.0°N, Leamington at 42.1°N, Hokkaido at 43.1°N) experience winter outdoor minimums of 4–10 mol/m²/day, requiring 160–280 µmol/m²/s of high-efficacy LED assimilation lighting and double aluminum energy screens to sustain fruiting crops.

2. Psychrometric Enthalpy, Bowen Ratio & Cooling Selection

In arid and semi-arid subtropics (e.g., Almería, Culiacán, Yuma, Riyadh, Dalby), summer DLI exceeds 50–60 mol/m²/day while ambient wet-bulb depression remains large ($\Delta T_{wb} > 10\text{ °C}$). Here, evaporative pad-and-fan systems or 70–100 bar high-pressure fogging achieve high adiabatic cooling efficiency. Conversely, in humid subtropical and tropical monsoon zones (e.g., Toyohashi, Gimje, Kunming, Cameron Highlands, Dalat), elevated ambient vapor pressure limits evaporative cooling potential, necessitating tall gutter heights (6.0–7.0 m), continuous ridge buoyancy ventilation, infrared-reflective shading screens, and active mechanical dehumidification.
